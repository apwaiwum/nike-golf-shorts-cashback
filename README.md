# nike golf shorts: How to Pick the Right Inseam, Fabric and Price, Plus Cashback on Every Pair

Most people shopping for nike golf shorts make the same mistake. They scroll through the grid, pick the colour they like, and order their usual waist size. Then the shorts show up, and either they're riding up on the 12th tee, or they're hanging below the knee like cargo pants from 2004.

Inseam is the number that actually decides whether a pair of golf shorts works for you, and it's buried in the product details on almost every listing. Here's what the current Nike golf lineup looks like, which pairs fit which kind of golfer, and how to claw back a few percent of what you spend.

## The three things that actually matter

**Inseam.** Nike sells golf shorts at roughly 16.5cm, 18cm and 20cm, which works out to about 6.5, 7 and 8 inches. There's also a longer 10.5-inch cut on some models. Shorter inseams sit above the knee and suit golfers who want full freedom on a deep squat into a putt. Longer ones read more traditional and are usually required at private clubs with dress codes.

**Fabric, and it splits into two families.** Knit shorts feel like a thick performance tee — soft, four-way stretch, quick to dry. Woven shorts feel more like a lightweight chino and hold a crisper crease. Nike's Par shorts are knit. The Tour chinos and the Jordan Sport shorts are woven.

**Waistband grip.** Sounds like a marketing bullet point until you've hit a drive with your shirt untucked. Several Nike golf shorts line the inner waistband with silicone gripper tape specifically so a tucked shirt stays tucked through the swing.

## Sizing notes that will change your order

Two things trip people up consistently.

The Tour chino is cut small. Nike says so on the product page. If you wear a 32 in the Victory, a 32 in the Tour chino will likely feel tight through the seat and waist. Go up one.

The Velocity is long. "Designed to land at your knees" is the product description, not a sizing chart quirk. If you buy it expecting the Victory's above-the-knee cut, you'll be sending it back.

Nike's standard return window is 30 days with free store pick-up and free returns, though exclusions apply. That matters more than usual here, because **returned and exchanged orders don't earn cashback**, and partial returns get prorated. Ordering two sizes to try both is a legitimate strategy — just know you'll only earn on what you keep.

## Five ways people lose their cashback

This is the part nobody reads until their balance doesn't move.

**They don't clear the cart first.** Nike requires an empty cart and a closed Nike app before you start your session, for every purchase. Leftover items from a previous browse can break tracking.

**They keep the Nike app open.** Starting on ShopBack and then completing checkout inside the Nike app is one of the most common failure modes. Finish on the site.

**They click something else mid-session.** A Facebook ad, a Google shopping result, another cashback extension, or a coupon site in another tab. Any of those can overwrite the tracking cookie.

**They run an ad blocker or a VPN.** Both are explicitly called out. Cashback simply won't track.

**They decline cookies.** Nike can only confirm a transaction to ShopBack if you accept all cookies on its pages.

If a payment fails partway through, restart the whole session from ShopBack rather than retrying in place.
